Google released TPU Sync, an open-source C++ library that transfers KV caches and model weights across TPU HBM, host DRAM, storage, and network infrastructure ([MarkTechPost](https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/11/13/google-tpu-sync-kv-cache-transfer-library/)). The library achieves over 90 percent lower KV transfer latency on MoE models ranging from 235B to 480B parameters, directly addressing inference bottlenecks in large-scale deployments. This represents a concrete engineering contribution to the open inference ecosystem rather than an architectural or model innovation.

Google also released Gemini 4 Hydrogen, which replaces the Flash series with a 1M-token context window, 4 levels of thinking, and pricing at $1.50 and $7.50 per million tokens ([MarkTechPost](https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/11/13/google-gemini-4-hydrogen/)). Hydrogen reports a 51 Artificial Analysis score and 57 percent performance on Terminal-Bench 4.1, and benchmarks indicate it nearly matches Claude Sonnet 5.5 while costing approximately 2.5 times more per task than GPT-6.1 Sol ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/googles-gemini-4-hydrogen-outscores-the-bigger-argon-just-four-weeks-after-its-launch/)). Additionally, CopilotKit released OpenBot 1.0, an MIT-licensed, self-hostable AI coworker workspace where organizations deploy their own agents and models while all actions pass policy checks and audit trails ([MarkTechPost](https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/11/12/copilotkit-openbot-1-0-open-source-ai-coworker/)), and llama.cpp reached v1.0.0 ([Hugging Face Blog](https://huggingface.co/blog/ggml-org/llama-cpp-v1)).
