Thinking Machines released Inkling, its first open-weight model, marking the company's public debut after spending 18 months building AI infrastructure largely outside public view. ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/15/thinking-machines-amps-up-its-bet-against-one-size-fits-all-ai-with-its-first-open-model-inkling/)) The release represents the company's proof point for its thesis that single monolithic models do not serve all use cases optimally.

In efficiency work, PrismML compressed a 27-billion-parameter reasoning model to under 4 GB, small enough to run on an iPhone, with the smallest version retaining 90 percent of original performance and minimal degradation on math and coding benchmarks. ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/bonsai-27b-is-a-full-open-reasoning-model-that-fits-on-an-iphone/)) Apple is reportedly already testing the compression technique. In related research, Hugging Face published technical posts on agent building via Shippy and model routing complexity.
