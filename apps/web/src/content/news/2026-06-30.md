OpenAI on June 26 previewed GPT-5.6, a three-tier model family designated Sol, Terra, and Luna, in a gated rollout restricted to roughly 20 partner organizations after the company shared its release plans with the U.S. government ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/previewing-gpt-5-6-sol/), [VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/technology/openai-unveils-gpt-5-6-sol-terra-and-luna-models-but-only-accessible-to-limited-preview-partners-for-now-per-us-gov)). Sol targets the hardest problems including complex coding and cybersecurity research, Terra handles high-volume business workflows, and Luna serves as a fast, low-cost tier for everyday automation ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/previewing-gpt-5-6-sol/)). Token pricing runs at $5 input and $30 output per million for Sol - matching the current GPT-5.5 flagship - $2.50/$15 for Terra, and $1/$6 for Luna. The family also revises prompt caching with explicit cache breakpoints, a 30-minute minimum cache lifetime, a 1.25x surcharge on cache writes, and a 90% discount on cache reads, making cost modeling more predictable for long-running agent pipelines ([OpenAI Help Center](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001325-a-preview-of-gpt-56-sol-terra-and-luna)). Broader API availability is planned for the weeks ahead.

June 30 closes the first full billing cycle under GitHub Copilot's usage-based model, which replaced flat-rate subscriptions on June 1, 2026 ([GitHub Blog](https://github.blog/news-insights/company-news/github-copilot-is-moving-to-usage-based-billing/)). Plans now bundle monthly AI Credits priced at $0.01 per credit: Pro ($10/month) includes $15 in credits, Pro+ ($39/month) $70, and Max ($100/month) $200, with Business and Enterprise customers receiving promotional credit boosts through September 1 ([GitHub Docs](https://docs.github.com/en/copilot/concepts/billing/usage-based-billing-for-individuals)). Developer reception since launch has been sharply negative: one Pro+ subscriber reported consuming roughly 8% of the 7,000-credit monthly allotment within two hours of agentic coding sessions, attributing the drain to multi-step agent workflows that generate far more tokens than the single-turn prompts the prior flat-rate model was sized around ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/30/what-a-joke-github-copilots-new-token-based-billing-spurs-consternation-among-devs/), [gHacks](https://www.ghacks.net/2026/06/02/github-copilot-usage-based-billing-takes-effect-drawing-developer-backlash-over-rapid-credit-depletion/)).

Google delayed the general availability of Gemini 3.5 Pro past its announced June target, pushing the launch to July after reviewing feedback from limited Vertex AI enterprise testing ([Bind AI](https://blog.getbind.co/gemini-3-5-pro-slips-to-july-and-four-senior-google-researchers-just-left-for-anthropic/)). Sundar Pichai had committed to a June GA window at Google I/O on May 20, with planned specifications that include a 2-million-token context window and a Deep Think reasoning mode ([Google Blog](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/gemini-3-5/)). The delay coincides with a significant loss of senior research talent: Noam Shazeer, Gemini co-lead and co-author of the original Transformer paper, departed for OpenAI on June 18 ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/06/18/google-gemini-co-lead-noam-shazeer-leaves-for-openai.html)), while AlphaFold project lead John Jumper and researchers Jonas Adler and Alexander Pritzel left for Anthropic within the same period ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/24/ai-researchers-continue-to-leave-google-for-its-rivals/), [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/06/23/google-deepmind-ai-researcher-departures-raise-doubts-about-ability-to-win-the-ai-race-shazeer-jumper-eye-on-ai/)).
