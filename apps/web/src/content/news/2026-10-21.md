Deepseek open-sourced DeepInfer, the vLLM-based inference framework behind its V4.1-Flash model, aiming to reduce compute costs and energy consumption when running AI models at scale ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/deepseek-open-sources-the-inference-engine-that-powers-its-fastest-and-cheapest-ai-model)). The release adds pricing pressure on Western providers by making efficient inference infrastructure available to developers building self-hosted systems.

Google Cloud, alongside Red Hat and IBM, released llm-d 1.0, an open-source Kubernetes-native stack for distributed LLM inference that adds multi-tenant flow control, zero-downtime upgrades, and agent-aware routing across vLLM, SGLang, TPUs, and GPUs ([MarkTechPost](https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/10/20/llm-d-1-0-open-source-distributed-inference-stack)). Separately, Mistral AI launched Voxtral 3, a family of speech-to-text and text-to-speech models paired with a LiveKit-based voice agent SDK that can run on self-hosted infrastructure and supports real-time tool integration across more than 50 languages ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/mistral-ai-launches-voxtral-3-a-full-voice-agent-stack-that-listens-thinks-and-talks-in-real-time)), while Elevenlabs released two new speech-to-speech models with 98.8 percent accuracy on Big Bench Audio and 0.57-second response time ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/elevenlabs-releases-two-new-speech-to-speech-models-designed-for-real-time-ai-conversations)).
