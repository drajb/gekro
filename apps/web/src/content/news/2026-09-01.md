OpenAI is offering outcome-based pricing to large customers, where payment occurs only after AI systems complete assigned tasks successfully ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/openai-starts-charging-some-customers-only-when-its-ai-actually-works/)). Salesforce, Adobe, and several startups are similarly moving away from fixed subscription models, reflecting shifts in how vendors price AI capability. The approach addresses long-standing disputes over credit attribution between software and user implementation.

China's ChangXin Memory Technologies (CXMT) has begun producing HBM3E memory chips in limited quantities, narrowing the supply gap for high-bandwidth memory used in current AI processors ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/chinas-cxmt-makes-its-first-hbm3e-chips-closing-the-ai-memory-gap/)). Separately, OpenClaw 2.0, an open-source AI platform, shipped with over 16,000 pull requests, adding cloud-based sessions on rented hardware and rebuilt browser functionality ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/openclaw-2-0-brings-simplified-setup-a-rebuilt-browser-app-and-multiplayer-sessions/)).
