SpaceX confirmed on June 16 a binding $60 billion all-stock agreement to acquire Anysphere, the company behind the AI coding assistant Cursor, days after SpaceX's IPO ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/16/spacex-to-acquire-cursor-for-60b-in-stock-days-after-blockbuster-ipo/), [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/06/16/spacex-spcx-cursor-acquisition-ipo.html)). The deal was first structured in April with a $10 billion break-up clause and is expected to close in the third quarter pending regulatory approvals ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-16/spacex-cements-60-billion-deal-to-take-over-ai-startup-cursor)). Cursor's annualized recurring revenue reached $4 billion in early June 2026, up from $2 billion in February ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/16/spacex-to-acquire-cursor-for-60b-in-stock-days-after-blockbuster-ipo/)). SpaceX has positioned the acquisition as strengthening its AI division, which is built around xAI following the two companies' merger earlier in 2026 ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/06/16/spacex-spcx-cursor-acquisition-ipo.html)).

Also on June 16, OpenAI published details of Deployment Simulation, a pre-release evaluation method that replays de-identified real user conversations through a candidate model to catch behavioral drift and reward hacking before a model reaches production ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/deployment-simulation/)). OpenAI validated the approach across roughly 1.3 million conversations spanning GPT-5 Thinking through GPT-5.4, collected between August 2025 and March 2026 ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/deployment-simulation/)). Separately, Google's Gemini CLI stops serving free, Pro, and Ultra tier users effective today, June 18, redirecting them to the new Antigravity CLI ([Google Developers Blog](https://developers.googleblog.com/an-important-update-transitioning-gemini-cli-to-antigravity-cli/), [GitHub Discussion](https://github.com/google-gemini/gemini-cli/discussions/27274)). Antigravity CLI is built in Go and supports asynchronous multi-agent workflows, but unlike Gemini CLI - which shipped under the Apache 2.0 license - it is closed source ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/ai-ml/2026/05/20/bye-bye-gemini-cli-google-nudges-devs-toward-antigravity/5243605)); the announcement thread on GitHub received 263 downvotes against 6 upvotes ([GitHub Discussion](https://github.com/google-gemini/gemini-cli/discussions/27274)). Enterprise users on Google Cloud Standard or Enterprise licenses retain Gemini CLI access ([GitHub Discussion](https://github.com/google-gemini/gemini-cli/discussions/27274)).
