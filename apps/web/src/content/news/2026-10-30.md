Researchers at Epoch AI demonstrated that the widely available open-weight model GLM 5.3 autonomously converted public Firefox security fixes into working exploits affecting six Firefox releases, each costing approximately $1,000 in API fees to generate and bypassing safeguards from multiple tested providers ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/open-weight-model-glm-5-3-built-full-firefox-exploit-chains-with-zero-human-input-in-under-48-hours/)). The exploits were produced without human input in under 48 hours, illustrating the capability gap between open-weight and frontier models on security-critical tasks.

Mistral released weights for Large 4, also known as "Le Chonk," a trillion-parameter model trained entirely in Europe ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/paris-based-mistral-releases-le-chonk-a-strong-coding-model-that-still-trails-china-overall/)). The model achieved strong performance on coding benchmarks relative to other open-weight alternatives and operates at competitive inference cost, though independent testing shows it trails leading Chinese models on overall capability metrics ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/29/mistral-releases-weights-for-its-trillion-parameter-ai-model-targeting-cybersecurity/)). Separately, Baseten released joey-1, a 2B open-weight decision model that returns calibrated probabilities at $0.01 per million input tokens with 20 ms P50 latency ([MarkTechPost](https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/10/29/baseten-joey-1-2b-decision-model/)), and Prime Intellect released Prime Mega MoE, a trainable fused MoE kernel achieving up to 3.11x forward pass speedup and 34% training step improvements on Qwen3 MoE ([MarkTechPost](https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/10/29/prime-intellect-prime-mega-moe-fused-moe-kernel-training/)).
