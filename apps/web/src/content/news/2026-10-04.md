Aleph Alpha has released Kolibri, a 78.1B-parameter English-German Mixture-of-Experts model that activates only 3.46B parameters per token ([MarkTechPost](https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/10/04/aleph-alpha-releases-kolibri-a-78-1b-open-weight-english-german-moe-model-with-only-3-46b-active-parameters/)). The model supports a 1M-token context window and per-request reasoning effort, with Apache 2.0 licensed FP8 weights capable of running on a single B200 or H200 GPU ([MarkTechPost](https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/10/04/aleph-alpha-releases-kolibri-a-78-1b-open-weight-english-german-moe-model-with-only-3-46b-active-parameters/)). The release targets practitioners seeking efficiency gains through conditional computation on inference-constrained hardware.

Google has restructured its Gemini access tiers, restricting free users to Flash-Lite, the smallest available model, while Flash and Pro models are reserved for paid subscribers ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/googles-new-gemini-tiers-cut-free-users-to-its-weakest-model-and-lock-5-month-subscribers-out-of-pro/)). The change, effective in October 2026, may signal preparation for launching Gemini 4 Argon, a more resource-intensive model ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/googles-new-gemini-tiers-cut-free-users-to-its-weakest-model-and-lock-5-month-subscribers-out-of-pro/)).
