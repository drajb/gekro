OpenAI is offering selected large customers, including Salesforce and Adobe, outcome-based pricing models where payment is contingent on task completion rather than fixed subscription fees ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/openai-starts-charging-some-customers-only-when-its-ai-actually-works/)). The shift reflects a broader industry trend toward performance-linked compensation structures, though the allocation of credit between software and user input remains contested.

Separately, the EU Commission has classified ChatGPT as a very large search engine under the Digital Services Act, citing at least 45 million monthly EU users ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/chatgpt-now-faces-stricter-eu-oversight-as-a-very-large-search-engine/)). OpenAI must submit risk assessments, transparency reports, and an advertising archive by the end of 2026 ([The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/986682/openai-chatgpt-eu-dsa)). Additionally, OpenClaw Foundation released version 2.0 of its open-source AI platform, featuring cloud sessions, real-time collaboration, and a rebuilt browser application ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/openclaw-2-0-brings-simplified-setup-a-rebuilt-browser-app-and-multiplayer-sessions/)).
