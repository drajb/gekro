Meta released Muse Glimmer, a 30B parameter open-weight agentic model under Apache 2.0 license that fits within 24 GB VRAM on consumer hardware and achieves 3.1x faster decoding with DFlash speculation ([MarkTechPost](https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/08/10/meta-ai-releases-muse-glimmer/)). The model was released by Meta's Superintelligence Labs and represents a return to open-source development after the company's shift toward closed models.

WebAI released TwIL-LM, a family of formal-logic models at 1.7B and 3B parameters that translate English into first-order logic and verify logical conclusions, with the 3B variant running on CPU or 4GB of VRAM and the 1.7B downloading at 1.06GB ([MarkTechPost](https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/08/10/webai-releases-twil-lm-a-1-7b-and-3b-formal-logic-model-family-for-autoformalization-on-local-hardware/)). Both models ship under a non-commercial license. Separately, OpenAI launched GPT-5.6-Cyber as part of its Daybreak cybersecurity program; the model answers up to 98.5 percent of security queries that would otherwise be blocked and has already uncovered two previously unknown Chrome vulnerabilities ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/openai-launches-gpt-5-6-cyber-to-help-defenders-find-vulnerabilities-before-attackers-do/)).
