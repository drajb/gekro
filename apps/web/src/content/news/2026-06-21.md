The U.S. Commerce Department's Bureau of Industry and Security issued an export control directive on June 12 requiring Anthropic to suspend access to its Fable 5 and Mythos 5 models for any foreign national, whether located inside or outside the United States, including non-citizen Anthropic employees ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/06/13/anthropic-disables-fable-mythos-export-controls-national-security-threat/), [Nextgov](https://www.nextgov.com/artificial-intelligence/2026/06/anthropic-suspends-top-ai-models-after-us-export-control-order/414173/)). The directive arrived three days after the models publicly launched on June 9 ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/06/13/anthropic-disables-fable-mythos-export-controls-national-security-threat/)). Officials cited a reported technique to bypass Fable 5's safety classifiers and access the advanced cybersecurity capabilities built into Mythos 5 - which can autonomously identify and exploit software vulnerabilities - as the basis for the restriction ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/06/13/anthropic-disables-fable-mythos-export-controls-national-security-threat/)). Unable to verify user nationality in real time, Anthropic disabled both models globally rather than attempting partial compliance ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/fable-mythos-access)). Anthropic characterized the jailbreak as narrow and limited to one specific scenario rather than a universal bypass of all safeguards, and said it is working toward restoring access ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/06/13/anthropic-disables-fable-mythos-export-controls-national-security-threat/), [Nextgov](https://www.nextgov.com/artificial-intelligence/2026/06/anthropic-suspends-top-ai-models-after-us-export-control-order/414173/)). Anthropic's updated privacy policy, effective July 8, adds provisions for collecting government-issued identity documents and biometric data to verify citizenship ([CIO](https://www.cio.com/article/4185510/anthropics-new-privacy-policy-offers-us-consumers-a-way-around-fable-ban.html), [Cybernews](https://cybernews.com/ai-news/anthropic-privacy-policy-id-verification/)); the anticipated system would restore access for verified U.S. citizens while international users remain blocked ([CIO](https://www.cio.com/article/4185510/anthropics-new-privacy-policy-offers-us-consumers-a-way-around-fable-ban.html)). Access to Claude Opus 4.8 and other existing Claude models was not affected by the directive ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/06/13/anthropic-disables-fable-mythos-export-controls-national-security-threat/)).

On June 18, the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission voted unanimously to order six major U.S. grid operators - PJM Interconnection, Midcontinent Independent System Operator, Southwest Power Pool, California ISO, ISO New England, and the New York Independent System Operator - to expedite interconnection requests from AI data centers and other large electricity consumers ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/18/ai-data-centers-just-got-a-government-mandated-fast-lane-to-the-grid/)). Each operator has 30 days to file a report on available generating capacity and 60 days to defend or revise regional electricity rates, with data centers required to cover their own interconnection costs ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/18/ai-data-centers-just-got-a-government-mandated-fast-lane-to-the-grid/)). The orders follow pressure from the Department of Energy, which flagged interconnection delays as a threat to U.S. AI competitiveness and urged FERC to act ([Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/data-centers/us-energy-regulator-to-order-grid-operators-to-expedite-ai-data-center-applications-says-projects-should-bring-their-own-power-or-cut-usage-during-high-demand)).
