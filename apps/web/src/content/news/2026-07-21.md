NVIDIA has released Cosmos 3 Edge, a 4-billion-parameter open world model designed to run locally on edge devices. The model enables robots and vision AI agents to understand their surroundings, perform real-time reasoning, and generate robot actions on-device without cloud dependencies ([MarkTechPost](https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/07/21/nvidia-releases-cosmos-3-edge-a-4b-parameter-open-world-model-that-reasons-and-generates-robot-actions-on-device/)).

In related infrastructure news, Google is developing "Frozen v2," a custom server chip that embeds Gemini's architecture directly into silicon. According to internal sources, the chip could be 6 to 10 times more efficient than current TPUs when deployed, with a target ship date of 2028 ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/googles-frozen-v2-chip-reportedly-bakes-geminis-architecture-directly-into-silicon-for-efficiency-gains/)). Separately, research from Xiaomi shows that data scale outpaced model size when training robot controllers - performance gains from 100,000 hours of motion capture data substantially exceeded improvements from increasing model parameters, though absolute success rates remain low ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/xiaomi-robotics-1-shows-that-more-data-beats-bigger-models-when-training-robots-to-move/)).
