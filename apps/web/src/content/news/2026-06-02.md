GitHub Copilot's usage model changed fundamentally on June 1, and for anyone running agentic workflows on top of it, this is the billing change that actually matters. Every Copilot plan is now on AI Credits - each credit is $0.01 and maps 1:1 to API token rates for whichever model serves your request, counting input, output, and cached tokens. Code completions are still included without drawing credits, which is the reassuring headline GitHub led with, but agent sessions, chat turns, and all Copilot Extensions calls are now metered against your monthly pool. Pro+ subscribers get $39/month in credits (matching their subscription price), and Pro gets $10 in credits on a $10 plan - numbers that look clean until you run a single hour-long agentic build and watch the meter tick. I haven't stress-tested this against my Pi cluster's Copilot usage yet, but the effective cost model for anyone doing serious agentic work just shifted. TechCrunch documented the backlash: 900 downvotes and 400 comments on GitHub's announcement thread within hours of the change going live, which makes sense - teams benchmarked Copilot's throughput under an unlimited-completion model, and that baseline is now invalid.

Two other things worth tracking from the past 36 hours. TechCrunch covered WindBorne's release of WeatherMesh 6 on June 1 - a transformer-based atmospheric model that now matches the European Centre for Medium-Range Weather Forecasts on several key variables, with their five-day forecast accuracy hitting what ECMWF's one-day compute used to achieve, running on consumer hardware instead of a supercomputer, fed by 400 active sensor balloons from 15 global sites. The architecture is the same next-token-prediction transformer pattern used in LLMs applied to atmospheric state prediction, and the fact that it outperforms decades of physics-model-based government systems is a concrete data point for where domain-specific fine-tuned transformers land when training data is structured and high-quality. Separately, OpenAI's Codex Pro 2x promotional period expired May 31 - teams on the $100/month Pro plan that were stress-testing Codex's agentic coding throughput under promo conditions now have half the effective capacity at the same price, so any infrastructure sizing assumptions built around those higher limits are stale as of this week.
