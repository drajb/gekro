Anthropic released Claude Sonnet 5 on June 30, 2026, a midtier model designed to bring near-flagship agentic capabilities to developers at lower cost ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/30/anthropic-launches-claude-sonnet-5-as-a-cheaper-way-to-run-agents/)). At launch the model is priced at $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens on an introductory basis through August 31; standard pricing afterward moves to $3 and $15 per million tokens respectively ([MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/06/30/anthropic-claude-sonnet-5/), [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-sonnet-5)). On a knowledge-work benchmark Sonnet 5 slightly outperforms Opus 4.8, while benchmarks for reasoning, tool use, and software coding show substantial improvement over its predecessor Sonnet 4.6 ([9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/06/30/anthropic-upgrades-claude-with-new-sonnet-5-model-details-here/)). The model ships as the default for Free and Pro users, with access extended to Max, Team, Enterprise, Claude Code, and the API from day one ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-sonnet-5)). Anthropic also reports improved resistance to prompt-injection attacks and lower rates of compliance with misuse requests relative to the prior version - properties directly relevant to teams running autonomous agent pipelines ([MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/06/30/anthropic-claude-sonnet-5/)).

Also on June 30, the U.S. Department of Commerce lifted export controls it had imposed on Anthropic's Fable 5 and Mythos 5 models, clearing the way for global access to resume ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/06/30/anthropic-says-trump-admin-has-lifted-export-controls-on-claude-fable-5-and-mythos-5.html), [The Washington Post](https://www.washingtonpost.com/technology/2026/06/30/white-house-drops-export-controls-anthropics-mythos-fable-ai-models/)). The controls had been imposed around June 13, requiring Anthropic to immediately suspend access for all foreign nationals - including its own employees - after the Commerce Department cited a jailbreak it said could enable the models to act as unrestricted cyber tools ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/06/13/anthropic-disables-fable-mythos-export-controls-national-security-threat/)). Anthropic had publicly contested that characterization, describing the jailbreak as specific to a single narrow scenario rather than a broad bypass of Fable 5's safety guardrails ([Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/anishasircar/2026/06/16/anthropic-disabled-fable-5-and-mythos-5-after-a-us-export-control-order-heres-what-happened/)). Both models are scheduled to return to Claude.ai, Claude Code, and the API starting July 2.
