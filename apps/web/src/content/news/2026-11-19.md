Google DeepMind released ([Google DeepMind](https://deepmind.google/blog/introducing-gemini-4-neon/)) Gemini 4 Neon, a frontier model optimized for software development and knowledge work. The model supports text, images, video, audio, and PDFs with a context window of one million tokens, priced at $3 per million input tokens and $15 per million output tokens ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/google-ships-gemini-4-neon-its-fourth-new-gemini-model-in-a-month/)). Neon ranks first on the AA Coding Agent Index ([MarkTechPost](https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/11/18/google-gemini-4-neon-release/)) and is nearly 2x faster than its sibling Carbon while outperforming it on most benchmarks ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/googles-latest-ai-model-is-faster-cheaper-and-smarter-than-its-flagship-predecessor-from-just-weeks-ago/)).

Separately, freely available OCR models have reduced digitization costs to under $130 per million pages, representing a fivefold decrease over 18 months ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/ocr-models-now-digitize-a-million-pages-for-under-130-as-costs-drop-fivefold-in-18-months/)). OpenAI researchers reported rising adoption of coding agents internally, with theoretical token costs now exceeding some researchers' base salaries, though teams remain reluctant to work without these tools despite concerns about "AI slop" output on routine tasks ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/openais-own-researchers-now-say-they-value-their-ai-tools-more-than-nearly-half-their-colleagues/)).
