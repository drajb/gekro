## How to play

The board has dark squares and white squares. A dark square can carry up to two numbers. The **amber number in its top right** is the total for the run of white squares going across, starting just to its right. The **blue number in its bottom left** is the total for the run going down, starting just below it. A diagonal line splits the two.

Fill every white square with a digit from 1 to 9 so that:

1. the digits in each run add up to that run's total, and
2. no digit appears twice in the same run. A digit may repeat in different runs.

For example, 2 squares with total 3 can only hold 1 and 2, and 3 squares with total 24 can only hold 7, 8 and 9.

Pick a size (5x5, 7x7 or 9x9, counting the dark first row and column) and a level (Easy, Medium or Hard). **New puzzle** deals a fresh one, **Restart** resets this one, and the page address carries the seed so you can send a friend the exact puzzle.

Controls:

- Tap a white square, then a number on the pad; tapping the same number clears it. The two runs through the selected square are tinted, amber for across and blue for down.
- **Notes** switches the pad to pencil marks. Placing a digit removes it from the notes in the same runs.
- **Erase** clears the square and **Undo** takes back the last change. On the keyboard, arrow keys move between white squares, **1** to **9** fill, **0** or **Backspace** erases, **N** switches notes, **U** undoes and **H** asks for a hint.
- **Show mistakes** is on to start with. A square that repeats a digit in a run, or sits in a run over its total or full and wrong, gets a dashed red border and a mark.
- **Check** marks wrong digits with a solid red border and a cross.
- **Hint** fills in one square and tells you why, or says so if one of your digits is wrong. A puzzle where you used a hint does not count toward your best time, even after a restart.

A tick appears beside a total when its run is full and right. Colour never works alone: the two totals differ by position and a diagonal, given digits are bold with a thick border, rule breaks have a dashed border and a mark, and hints have a diamond. Screen readers hear each square's position, digit or notes, and the totals of its two runs. Nothing flashes, and the win animation is off under reduced motion.

## How it works

### Making a puzzle with one answer

1. **Pattern.** Row 0 and column 0 are dark. Inside, squares are made dark at random and the pattern is tidied: a lone white square in a run of 1 turns dark, runs longer than the level allows are cut, and only the largest connected white piece is kept. Easy allows runs of up to 4, Medium up to 6 and Hard has no cap.
2. **Digits.** The white squares are filled with digits that differ within every run, by a backtracking search that tries digits in random order.
3. **One answer.** A solver counts the solutions, stopping at two. If there are two, the page picks a square where a second solution differs from the intended answer and copies the correct digit in as a given. It repeats until there is exactly one solution.
4. **Level.** Easy then adds givens until a quarter of the white squares have one. Medium keeps only the givens it needed. Hard tries to remove each given again and keeps only those whose removal brings back a second solution.

Given digits over seeds 1 to 20:

| Level | 5x5 | 7x7 | 9x9 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Easy | 2 to 7 | 3 to 5 | 5 to 9 |
| Medium | 1 to 7 | 5 to 14 | 8 to 12 |
| Hard | 2 to 6 | 5 to 13 | 7 to 22 |

### The solver

For each run the solver knows every set of different digits that can make its total. There are 511 non-empty subsets of 1 to 9, each filed by its size and its sum. A run keeps only digits that appear in some set that still fits what each square can be. A square down to one digit is removed from the rest of its run. The two runs through a square pass what they learn to each other until nothing changes, and then the solver tries each digit of the square with the fewest options.

### Hints

When the other squares in a run are full, the last one is a subtraction, and the hint says so. When a total allows only one set of digits, it names the set. Otherwise it says that the across and down runs together leave one choice, or, failing that, reveals a square.

### What the tests check

- The digit-set table against all 511 subsets of 1 to 9, and the run geometry against an independently written scan.
- The solver's count of solutions against an independent counter that checks each run by brute force. They agree on hand-made boards (one answer, two answers, none) and on over a hundred generated boards with givens removed or a total nudged.
- Every generated puzzle has exactly one solution by the independent counter, totals that equal the sums of the stored answer, one connected white piece, and runs within the level's length cap. In a Hard puzzle, taking away any single given gives more than one solution.
- A hint is always the right digit for an empty square, and following hints from a fresh puzzle fills in the whole answer.

## Privacy and storage

Nothing is uploaded and there is no account. Your best time for each size and level is saved in your browser's local storage as small whole numbers in a small JSON object, and anything read back is checked before use. The only other state is the page address.

## Limits

- Boards stop at 9x9, so the longest run is 8 squares.
- The score is time, which starts on your first change and pauses when the tab is hidden.
- On a 9x9 board pencil notes are small. They are easier to read on a wider screen.
- Levels describe the pattern and the given digits, not how deep the reasoning is.

Everything runs locally in your browser.
